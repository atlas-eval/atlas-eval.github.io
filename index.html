A diagnostic evaluation framework for industrial tool-use agents

ATLAS: Dual-Horizon Diagnostic Evaluation for Tool-Use Agents in Meituan Xiaotuan

Abstract

LLM-based assistants on web-scale service platforms address local-service needs through multi-step planning and tool use. Under dynamic business conditions and evolving user needs, outcome-level evaluation offers limited evidence of where execution falls short, which quality or compliance requirement is unmet, or how service loses alignment over continued interaction.

ATLAS diagnoses two complementary horizons: within-turn, trajectory-wise signals examine the complete agent lifecycle triggered by a request across thinking and reflection, tool and skill execution, and response generation; across-turn, user-wise signals examine whether intent, constraints, and corrections carry forward across multiple lifecycles.

Rule-based checks and LLM judgments ground each signal in explicit evidence scopes and decision boundaries. LLM judges are calibrated on dynamically maintained business-log references, and selected judges are distilled into grouped compact models. Diagnostic profiles help developers attribute failures, prioritize workflow, data, or policy interventions, and reassess the resulting agent.

In Meituan Xiaotuan, a million-scale DAU local-services assistant, calibrated judges and distilled models achieve high diagnostic fidelity. A blinded human audit and a 14-day online A/B test with nearly two million users per arm confirm improved recommendation quality, answer read-through (+2.84%), and paid orders (+6.13%), leading to full production deployment.

Why evaluation must diagnose

Outcome reporting alone is not enough for targeted iteration.

Production tool-use agents combine reasoning, tool and skill execution, and responses grounded in live business supply. When service falls short, the cause may lie in the workflow, a tool interface, scenario coverage, or the policy—not just the final answer.

With limited engineering and training resources, teams need diagnostic evidence to locate bottlenecks, choose focused interventions, and assess the resulting changes.

What outcome reporting hidesA final outcome may reveal that service failed without showing the responsible execution stage, the unmet quality or compliance requirement, or where alignment with user context was lost.

Diagnostic evaluation should answer:

  1. 01Where does service fail?
  2. 02What should be prioritized?
  3. 03Did the intervention work?

Xiaotuan, Meituan’s AI assistant for local services, makes this need concrete. It interprets open-ended requests, retrieves live business information, and adjusts its actions using tool feedback before presenting grounded recommendations. The example illustrates the workflow behind the user-facing response.

  • Challenge 01 — Early errors can propagate across execution. A mistake in interpreting the request, rewriting a query, or choosing a tool can lead to an off-target response. The final answer alone does not reveal whether the cause lies in synthesis or an earlier execution step.
  • Challenge 02 — Quality is multifaceted under dynamic supply. Merchant inventory, pricing, and availability change continuously, and open-ended requests admit multiple valid paths. A useful recommendation must jointly satisfy user constraints, supply facts, deal timeliness, logical consistency, and safety requirements rather than match a fixed reference answer.
  • Challenge 03 — User context can drift across requests. An ongoing interaction is more than a sequence of isolated requests. Each later turn may appear reasonable on its own while overlooking an earlier reference, constraint, or correction; over time, these locally plausible decisions can drift away from the user’s evolving goal.

Why dual-horizonThe first two challenges concern the current request trajectory: ATLAS’s within-turn horizon inspects the execution and its use of current business evidence. The third concerns continued service: its across-turn horizon evaluates user-relevant state across complete lifecycles.

ATLAS’s central design

Two horizons for diagnosing agent service.

ATLAS preserves two complementary views: the full trajectory of one request and the evolving user context across successive lifecycles. Rather than reporting only an overall outcome, it retains the evidence needed to locate and interpret deficiencies.

Why the separation mattersWithin-turn evaluation localizes a capability deficiency in an execution. Across-turn evaluation asks whether subsequent service remains responsive as user-relevant context evolves.

Horizon 01 · Within-turn / trajectory-wise

Localize a gap in one request.

Examine the complete trajectory initiated by a request: thinking and reflection, tool or skill execution, updates after tool feedback, and response generation.

Diagnostic questionWhere does a capability deficiency arise in this execution?

Horizon 02 · Across-turn / user-wise

Diagnose continuity across requests.

Examine whether referents, intent, constraints, and explicit corrections are carried forward, updated, and used across complete lifecycles.

Diagnostic questionDoes later service still respond to the user’s evolving context?

Within-turn diagnostic structure

How the within-turn horizon localizes a gap.

Within a request, ATLAS records both the execution stage where behavior occurs and the capability requirement it fails to meet. These two axes organize the within-turn diagnostic matrix.

Across-turn stays outside this matrix by design: it evaluates relations among complete lifecycles and the evolving user context.

Trace a symptom to its source.

A user asks for non-dessert cakes, such as fried-chicken cakes or beef cakes. The agent reinterprets these as egg burgers and beef pies, searches for the substituted items, and presents the resulting supply as a match. The final response reflects the retrieved items, but those items no longer preserve the original request.

ATLAS starts from the visible recommendation mismatch and follows the evidence upstream: response-level Supply Relevance flags the symptom, while Rewrite Validity and Search Intent Understanding locate the earlier query-rewrite and intent-understanding errors. The distinction helps developers target the execution bottleneck rather than only rewrite the final answer.

From diagnosis to iteration

From diagnostic signals to targeted iteration.

ATLAS turns evidence-bound signals into a diagnostic profile. Developers use that profile to select workflow, data, or policy interventions, then reassess the agent. Rolling calibration and grouped distillation make the signals reliable and practical at scale; selected signals can also provide gated training feedback when policy optimization is the chosen intervention.

The semantic threadA signal’s target, evidence scope, and decision boundary are established before calibration and retained when the resulting policy is evaluated again.

  1. 01
    Specify the signal

    Each signal binds a specific behavior to admissible evidence and a decision rule. Programmatic checks handle deterministic cases; calibrated LLM judges handle semantic ones. Outputs are pass/fail by default, while quantitative signals are derived from local judgments and normalized.

  2. 02
    Calibrate and maintain the boundary

    Dynamically maintain about 1,000 references per LLM-based diagnostic signal from a rolling six-month log window. Stratify by business scenario, request complexity, and interaction type; split by original conversation into calibration and held-out test sets. Refine on the calibration split, then freeze the interface before testing.

  3. 03
    Distill compatible signals together

    Group selected signals by execution dimension, capability concern, and input–output form. Teacher-supervised compact models retain calibrated decisions while avoiding fragmented per-signal deployments or one shared model with incompatible evidence forms.

  4. 04
    Choose an intervention, then reassess

    Diagnostic profiles guide human-in-the-loop workflow refinement, targeted data curation, or policy optimization. The Xiaotuan iteration reuses selected signals as GRPO feedback with Norms & Compliance as a hard training-time gate; reassessment combines diagnostic replay, independent human auditing, and online A/B testing.

A diagnostic result is not an automatic product decision. It identifies a candidate capability deficiency; product context and deployment constraints still determine priority and intervention.

Experimental setting

Datasets come from de-identified Xiaotuan production logs over a six-month window and are separated by original conversation. The diagnostic benchmark covers 41 LLM-based signals with about 1,000 instances per signal, split 1:1 for calibration and held-out testing. Grouped Qwen3.5-9B models cover 17 selected signals. The downstream study optimizes Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on about 10,000 requests and uses 1,000 paired queries for blinded human review; supplementary diagnostic replay covers 2,000 requests.

Supplementary evidence: pre-deployment diagnostic replay 2,000 production requests

Where does execution improve?

Each agent executes 2,000 conversation-disjoint production requests through production interfaces against live business supply. The ATLAS-guided policy has the highest mean score in every reported group, with the largest gain in Tool & Skill Execution.

Replay also shows improved user-wise performance and a high Norms & Compliance score. These are ATLAS diagnostic scores, not an independent quality assessment; the blinded human audit and online A/B test provide that complementary validation.

Conclusion

Evaluation as evidence for targeted iteration.

ATLAS is a dual-horizon diagnostic framework for industrial tool-use agents. Trajectory-wise signals localize capability gaps in a current execution; user-wise signals assess whether subsequent service remains responsive as interaction context evolves.

Evidence-bound signals, rolling reference calibration, and grouped compact models make diagnosis executable at scale. Diagnostic profiles guide human-in-the-loop workflow, data, and policy interventions; the framework then reassesses the updated agent.

On Xiaotuan, calibrated judge fidelity, compact-model distillation, blinded human auditing, and a 14-day online A/B test connect diagnosis to improved recommendation quality, engagement, and paid orders. The ATLAS-guided agent is deployed to all production traffic.
